# Board representation for the search-free policy

**Question.** Which lossless board representation should the supervised policy use as its input? Five design axes were open: token layout (one token per square vs one per piece), content–position binding (embedding sum vs a joint lookup table), position information (learned embeddings vs fixed 2D sinusoidal vs 2D rotary), symmetry (none vs side-to-move canonicalization vs mirror augmentation), and metadata injection (side/castling/en-passant/counters as suffix tokens vs broadcast into every board token).

**Method.** Four sequential rounds, one axis each, with the other axes frozen at their incumbents; then a cross-confirmation round in which each round's runner-up was upgraded with all later winners, and a final held-out replication. Models were ~87M-parameter 12-layer transformers trained on nested subsets of Stockfish-annotated (depth 16) human-game positions (131k/262k/524k/992k unique positions, two passes each, identical update schedules; all arms within 0.06% parameter count). Primary metrics: cross-entropy and top-1 accuracy restricted to legal moves, on 8,192 held-out positions, with paired bootstrap intervals; training-fit probes and internal-health panels (attention entropy, effective rank, dead units) accompanied every run. The held-out replication used a further 8,192 positions disjoint from every position used in training or selection.

**Evidence.**

- Pieces layout beat squares decisively; doubling data did not close the gap (squares-additive at 992k positions still lost to pieces-additive at 524k).
- Learned position embeddings beat the fixed 2D sinusoidal grid by a wide margin at all scales; 2D rotary tied learned embeddings at 524k and lost significantly at 992k.
- Canonicalizing every position to the mover's perspective (rank mirror plus color swap, with the original side kept as a flag and logits mapped back exactly) was the largest single effect of the study: −0.156 nats and +1.9 top-1 points at 992k. Randomly mirroring training samples instead (augmentation) was a wash: it halved the train–validation gap but fit correspondingly slower, and this regime (two passes) does not overfit.
- Metadata as suffix tokens beat broadcast injection by a wide margin; broadcast measurably flattened input-side representation diversity (layer-0 effective rank 178 vs 314).
- Cross-confirmation overturned one round: with canonicalization in place, the joint content–position table beats additive binding (−0.036 nats, +1.1 top-1 points), reversing the pre-canonicalization ordering. Canonicalization turns piece colors into mover-relative roles, doubling the effective frequency of every joint-table row.
- The held-out set replicated the selection-set numbers almost exactly (2.598 vs 2.596 nats for the winner), showing no sign of selection overfitting.

**Conclusion.** The settled representation is: one token per piece, a learned joint piece×square table as the sole carrier of both content and position, side-to-move canonicalization, and metadata as suffix tokens. A standard-chess subtlety surfaced during implementation: the exact symmetry group is only Z2 (rank mirror with color swap) — a file mirror is not exact whenever castling rights exist, since castling is defined for a king on the e-file. All conclusions hold at equal update counts in a non-overfitting toy regime (~87M parameters, two passes); evaluation rankings tracked training-fit rankings throughout, so the evidence supports "best overall package at equal updates" rather than a cleanly separated inductive-bias advantage. Final appeal belongs to hero scale.
